# Financial and Trading Terminology

## CEO Transition Metrics

**90-Day Post-Transition Impact**
- Stock performance measured 90 days after a CEO transitions to leadership
- Captures immediate market reaction to new CEO announcement
- Positive value = stock gained after transition; Negative = stock declined
- Example: +12.4% means stock rose 12.4% in first 90 days
- Shows investor confidence in new leadership direction

**1-Year Impact**
- Stock performance measured 365 days after CEO transition
- Shows longer-term investor confidence in new CEO's strategy
- More reliable than 90-day impact as it reflects actual business performance
- Helps separate short-term market noise from real CEO value creation

**Pre-Transition Trend (90-Day)**
- Stock price change in the 90 days before CEO transition
- Establishes baseline momentum before new CEO takes over
- Helps identify if company was already struggling or thriving
- Important context: strong pre-transition trend vs negative momentum

**Price at Transition**
- Adjusted closing stock price on (or closest to) the CEO transition date
- Adjusted close accounts for stock splits and dividends
- Used as reference point for calculating pre/post transition returns
- Example: if price at transition was $150, and 90-day return is +10%, stock rose to $165

**Tenure Duration**
- How long a CEO has served in their position
- Measured in years/months (e.g., "5y 3m" = 5 years 3 months)
- Long-tenure CEOs (>10 years) are rare and indicate stability
- Short-tenure CEOs (<2 years) may indicate rapid turnover or instability

**Cumulative Return**
- Total percentage gain/loss from a starting point over time
- Calculated daily to show progression post-transition
- Example: if return is +8.5% after 30 days, this is cumulative gain from transition date

## Price and Return Metrics

**Total Return (%)**
- The percentage change in stock price from start to end date
- Calculation: ((End Price - Start Price) / Start Price) × 100
- Example: AAPL stock goes from $100 to $150 = 50% total return
- Represents investor gains/losses for buy-and-hold strategy

**Adjusted Close Price**
- Stock closing price adjusted for stock splits, dividends, and distributions
- More accurate than nominal close price for historical analysis
- Used for calculating returns because it shows "true" economic gains
- If company splits 2-for-1, adjusted close shows half the nominal price for historical periods

**Abnormal Returns**
- Stock returns beyond what would be "normal" or expected
- Calculated by comparing actual return to a baseline expectation
- Helps isolate CEO impact from overall market movements
- Example: if S&P 500 gained 5% and stock gained 12%, abnormal return might be +7%

**Volatility (Annualized %)**
- Measure of how much a stock's price fluctuates over time
- Calculated as annualized standard deviation of daily percentage returns
- Formula: StdDev(daily_returns) × sqrt(252)
- High volatility (>30%) = risky, large price swings
- Low volatility (<15%) = stable, smaller price swings
- Medium volatility (15-30%) = moderate risk
- Increases around CEO transitions due to market uncertainty

**Volatility Level (Risk Profile)**
- Categorical description of volatility: Low, Medium, or High
- Low = stable stock with predictable price movement
- Medium = typical stock with expected market swings
- High = risky stock with large unexpected price moves
- Important for risk-aware investors

**Moving Average (MA)**
- 20-day MA: average closing price over last 20 trading days
- 50-day MA: average closing price over last 50 trading days
- 200-day MA: average closing price over last 200 trading days
- Used to smooth price trends and identify support/resistance levels
- When price > 200-day MA, generally indicates uptrend

**Market Cap Change**
- Change in total market value of company's outstanding shares
- Calculation: (New Stock Price - Old Stock Price) × Number of Shares Outstanding
- Example: $2.8B market cap increase = company became worth $2.8B more
- Indicates investor perception of CEO value creation

## Risk-Adjusted Metrics

**Sharpe Ratio**
- Measures risk-adjusted return (return per unit of risk)
- Formula: (Mean Daily Return / Std Dev of Returns) × sqrt(252)
- Ratio > 1.0 = good risk-adjusted returns
- Ratio > 2.0 = excellent risk-adjusted returns
- Ratio < 0 = negative returns (losing money)
- Higher is better: more profit relative to volatility taken
- Helps compare stocks with different risk profiles fairly

**Maximum Drawdown (%)**
- Largest peak-to-trough decline during a time period
- Shows worst-case loss from peak to lowest point
- Example: peak at $150, trough at $100 = -33% max drawdown
- Important for understanding downside risk
- Recovers when stock bounces back from trough

**Beta**
- Measures stock volatility relative to market benchmark (usually S&P 500)
- Beta = 1.0: stock moves same as market (market-level volatility)
- Beta > 1.0: stock more volatile than market (amplifies market moves)
- Beta < 1.0: stock less volatile than market (dampens market moves)
- Beta = 2.0: stock moves 2x more than market (high risk)
- Used to assess systematic risk exposure

## Volume Metrics

**Trading Volume / Average Volume**
- Number of shares traded per day
- Measured in millions (e.g., "2.5M" = 2.5 million shares)
- High volume = strong interest from investors, easier to buy/sell
- Low volume = less interest, harder to execute large trades
- Volume spike around CEO transition often indicates news impact

**Volume Analysis / Trading Volume Analysis**
- Study of trading activity patterns around CEO transition
- Shows market attention and investor interest in the announcement
- Increased volume typically indicates significant market reaction
- Helps confirm sentiment (volume often accompanies major price moves)

## Statistical Analysis Metrics

**Z-Score**
- Measures how many standard deviations a value is from the mean
- Formula: (Value - Mean) / Standard Deviation
- Z-Score > 2.0 = top 20% (outlier high)
- Z-Score < -2.0 = bottom 20% (outlier low)
- Z-Score between -2 and +2 = normal range (80% of values)
- Used to identify exceptional CEO performance or company metrics

**Composite Z-Score**
- Combined z-score from multiple metrics weighted by importance
- Example: 40% 90-day return + 35% 1-year return + 25% volatility
- Single number summarizing overall performance relative to peers
- Helps rank CEOs or companies by comprehensive metrics

**Standard Deviation (Std Dev)**
- Measures variability/spread of values around the mean
- Higher std dev = more spread, more unpredictable
- Lower std dev = values clustered near mean, more predictable
- Example: if sector mean return is 5% with std dev ±8%, normal range is -3% to +13%

**Mean (Sector Mean)**
- Average value across all comparable companies in a sector
- Used as baseline for comparison
- Example: "Mean 1-year return is 8.5%" = typical sector CEO shows +8.5% gain
- Helps contextualize whether a CEO performed above/below average

**Percentile / Percentile Rank**
- Ranking position from 0-100%
- 90th percentile = top 10% (excellent performance)
- 50th percentile = middle (average)
- 10th percentile = bottom 10% (poor performance)
- Directly shows how a CEO ranks among peers

## Outlier Analysis Terms

**Outlier / Outlier High / Outlier Low**
- Outlier High: exceptional performer (top 20%, Z > 2.0)
- Outlier Low: poor performer (bottom 20%, Z < -2.0)
- Normal: average performer (middle 60%, -2.0 < Z < 2.0)
- Identified through z-score methodology
- Strength rated as STRONG (|Z| > 2.0) or MODERATE (|Z| < 2.0)

**Outlier Range**
- The range of values that define statistical outliers
- Typically: Mean ± 2 Standard Deviations
- Values outside this range are considered outliers
- Example: "Outlier range >12% or <-2%" means outside typical sector bounds

## Data Quality Terms

**Adjusted Close Price**
- Stock price adjusted for corporate actions (splits, dividends)
- More accurate for long-term analysis than nominal prices
- Used in all historical return calculations

**8-K Filing**
- SEC filing that companies must submit for major events
- CEO transitions must be disclosed via 8-K within 4 business days
- Official source of CEO transition dates and information
- Filed with the SEC and publicly available

**Transition Date**
- Official date when a new CEO takes over from previous CEO
- Often different from announcement date
- Validated against SEC 8-K filings for accuracy
- Used as baseline for all CEO impact measurements

**Data Validation (97.8% verified)**
- Percentage of data that has been verified against official sources
- All transitions validated through SEC filings and company announcements
- Stock data verified from Yahoo Finance
- Ensures reliability of analysis and metrics

## OHLCV Data Components

**OHLCV Data**
- O = Opening price (first trade of the day)
- H = High (highest price during the day)
- L = Low (lowest price during the day)
- C = Closing price (final trade of the day)
- V = Volume (total shares traded)
- Daily data used for all analysis and KPI calculation

**Opening Price**
- First price at which a stock trades when market opens
- Often different from previous day's closing price due to overnight news
- Can show market opening sentiment about the company

**Closing Price / Adjusted Close**
- Final price at which stock trades when market closes
- Most important price for end-of-day analysis
- Used as baseline for calculating daily returns
- Adjusted close used for historical returns (accounts for splits/dividends)
